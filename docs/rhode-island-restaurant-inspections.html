Checking a Rhode Island restaurant inspection

Rhode Island has one food inspector: the Department of Health's Center for Food Protection, for every city and town. Its reports are public, but they aren't published as data, and they carry no score, grade or placard: they list the violations found and when each must be corrected.

Search the Department of Health's reports

Reading a report

Inspections follow the Rhode Island Food Code. A report lists each violation by its Food Code item, marks the ones corrected during the visit, and gives a date to correct the rest. Some items are foodborne-illness risk factors; a report with more of those deserves a closer read, but there's no official score to compare.

Rhode Island Eats shows no inspection results and computes no grade of its own. The search is for looking places up one at a time and offers no download, so we link it rather than copy it. One inspection is a snapshot of one day.

What Rhode Island Eats does show

Every place in the app and on the web search links to the state's inspection search. Our lists come from open map data, checked against the Department of Health's own (2018) license list, with RIGIS's 2025 list of licensed food markets marking grocery and convenience counters. The rest is hand-checked research and James Beard honors.
